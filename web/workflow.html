Stage 1 of 5: Capture

A manageable first project.

The first tool could cover intake through to the estimating handoff. We would agree on that scope, try it with staff, and see whether it makes the work easier before extending it to other tasks.

Choosing the approach

What would we actually build?

First, look at what your current software can already do. A better setup or a simpler process may be enough. When there is a gap, these are possible ways to fill it.

Automation

Connect repeatable steps.

A submitted form could create a request and flag missing fields. A reminder could bring overdue follow-up back to staff attention.

In this example: move information and prompt the next action using clear rules.

Custom software

Give the work a home.

A focused application could show the request, photos, visit notes, responsibility, and status together when existing tools do not fit.

In this example: build the shared record and views staff need.

Practical AI

Help prepare information.

If long messages take time to sort through, AI could draft a summary for staff to compare with the original. Unprovided details stay marked as missing.

In this example: an optional draft summary, checked by a person.

Working together

From the first conversation
to everyday use.

You bring the work and the people who understand it. I help turn that understanding into a focused improvement.

  1. Walk through the work.

    Show me a recent example, including where it got awkward. We identify the people, information, handoffs, and improvement that matters.

    You get: a clear problem and a practical recommendation.

  2. Agree on a first version.

    Most projects are flat fee, with the scope and project price agreed before work begins. We also agree on responsibilities, timing, data ownership, recurring costs, and support. Any additional work and cost are agreed before proceeding.

    You get: a written scope and an agreed project price, with a prototype included when it is part of the plan.

  3. Try it with real tasks.

    The people doing the work test the agreed tool in a limited pilot. We check ordinary jobs, missing information, changes, and what to do when something fails.

    You get: a working tool checked against the agreed workflow.

  4. Put it to use and review.

    I help staff get started, provide the agreed handover information, and compare the result with the old process. Further improvements can be scoped from there.

    You get: clear ownership, instructions, and agreed support arrangements.

How do we know it helped?

For the intake example, compare time spent gathering details, requests without a next action, and whether staff can prepare an estimate from the record. Use observations from the pilot to judge the improvement.
